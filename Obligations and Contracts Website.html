Article 1156 • Civil Code of the Philippines

Definition of an Obligation

"An obligation is a juridical necessity to give, to do or not to do."

Juridical Necessity: In case of non-compliance, the courts of justice may be called upon to enforce performance or recover damages for breach.

Module 1 Four Requisites

Essential Elements (Requisites) of an Obligation

1. Active Subject

Creditor / Obligee

The person who has the legal right to demand the performance of the obligation.

2. Passive Subject

Debtor / Obligor

The person bound to perform the prestation (to give, to do, or not to do).

3. Object / Prestation

Subject Matter

The conduct or performance required (giving a thing, rendering service, or refraining from an act).

4. Juridical Tie

Efficient Cause

The legal relation created by law, contract, quasi-contract, delict, or quasi-delict.

Module 2 Art. 1157

The 5 Sources of Obligations

1. LAW (Ex Lege)

Imposed directly by statutory provisions and cannot be presumed.

Ex: Obligation to pay taxes under the Tax Code.
2. CONTRACTS (Ex Contractu)

Arise from the meeting of minds and have the force of law between parties (Art. 1159).

Ex: Commercial loan agreement or lease.
3. QUASI-CONTRACTS

Lawful, voluntary, and unilateral acts to prevent unjust enrichment (Art. 2142).

Ex: Solutio Indebiti (wrong payment) & Negotiorum Gestio.
4. DELICTS (Ex Maleficio)

Civil liabilities arising from criminal offenses under the Revised Penal Code (Art. 100 RPC).

Ex: Obligation to pay restitution after theft conviction.
5. QUASI-DELICTS (Torts)

Acts or omissions causing damage through fault or negligence where no contract exists (Art. 2176).

Ex: Vehicular collision caused by reckless driving.
Module 3 Primary Classification

Primary Kinds of Obligations

Pure vs. Conditional Obligations

Pure obligations are demandable at once. Conditional obligations depend upon a future and uncertain event (Suspensive or Resolutory).

Joint vs. Solidary Obligations

Joint obligations divide debt proportionally among co-debtors. Solidary obligations allow demanding full debt from any single debtor.

Alternative vs. Facultative Obligations

Alternative: Debtor can perform one of several prestations. Facultative: Only one prestation is due, but a substitute may be rendered.

Obligations with a Penal Clause

Contains an accessory undertaking (penalty) to guarantee enforcement and fix liquidated damages in case of breach.

Module 4 Art. 1231 Modes

Modes of Extinguishing Obligations

1. Payment or Performance Complete delivery of the thing or rendering of service due.
2. Loss of the Thing Due Physical loss, destruction, or legal impossibility without debtor's fault.
3. Condonation / Remission Gratuitous forgiveness or abandonment of debt by the creditor.
4. Confusion or Merger When qualities of creditor and debtor are united in the same person.
5. Compensation When two persons in their own right are reciprocal creditors and debtors of each other.
6. Novation Modification or substitution of an obligation by a new valid one.
Module 5 Art. 1305 & Defective Contracts

Contracts & Defective Contracts Comparison

Essential Requisites of Contracts (Art. 1318)

No contract exists unless three elements concur: (1) Consent of contracting parties, (2) Object Certain which is the subject matter, and (3) Cause of the obligation established.
